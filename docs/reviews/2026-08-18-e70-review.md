## Big picture

**The per-class pattern is genuine domain shift, not a label-map or grammar artifact.**
Three independent lines of evidence: (1) the weak classes (Incision 0.51, Viscoelastic
0.42, Hydro 0.37, rhexis 0.68) are all high-confidence 1:1 map entries while the merged
classes are the strong ones — a map artifact would show the opposite; (2) argmax
per-class F1 ≈ Viterbi per-class F1 — the collapse exists before the grammar touches
anything, and Viterbi did exactly its deployment job (edit 55.6→71.3, seg_ratio
1.66→1.08) without moving class recall; (3) the dominant error is **idle-attraction**:
1537/8282 (18.6%) of non-idle frames predicted idle, worst for Incision (332/529 = 63%).
Combined with the 3–6× tool-feature attenuation, the mechanism: short phases
discriminated by thin-instrument identity (keratome, cystotome, hydro cannula —
unfamiliar Brest instruments the C1K seg model barely detects) fall back to "no tool
seen ⇒ idle," while long phases with distinctive global appearance survive. The
rhexis→hydro confusion (200 frames, 25%) fits — Brest bent-needle cystotome vs C1K
forceps-dominant rhexis.

**Should the weak early phases join the falsification probe? No.** The probe's job is
triggering *map surgery*; the early-phase collapse is a *training-data* problem that
E7-b is designed to fix, and those classes already carry full weight in the primary
endpoint. What E7-0 does justify: findings 3 and 4. **The success bar stands** — 0.678
leaves ample headroom.

E7-0 delivered exactly what a baseline should: the frozen model does not trivially
transfer (0.954→0.678), so the augmentation experiment is worth its GPU-hours, and the
per-class breakdown predicts where E7-b's gains must come from. If E7-b lifts CATARACTS
macro-F1 mainly by fixing idle-attraction without touching C1K polishing/flushing,
that's the clean win scenario.
